Have you ever recruited volunteers who were genuinely excited about your work, and then a month later you were chasing them to show up?

If that sounds familiar, I want you to look at the experience you’re giving them. In building AREAi, I’ve seen how much it matters to give people meaningful responsibilities and opportunities to develop. And I think we sometimes forget to do that with volunteers because they already believe in the cause.

Think about the last time you told a volunteer, “We need you to help us with outreach.” What does that mean to somebody who has a job, school or family commitments? How much time are you asking for? Who’s going to show them what to do? What happens after they do it?

Here’s how I would make the same request.

“We’re trying to understand why some parents missed our last session. Could you speak to five parents this week? I’ll share the questions and walk through the first call with you. It should take about an hour. What day would work for you?”

Then come back to them. Say, “Three parents told us they couldn’t leave work in time, so we’re trying a Saturday session next month.” And if that volunteer wants to do more, invite them to help plan it. They already understand why you’re making the change.

If you want to improve volunteer retention, start with one role this week. Give the person an assignment they can own, check that it fits their time, and tell them what their contribution helped you do. That follow-up takes two minutes. Make time for it.
